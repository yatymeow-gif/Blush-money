🚀 Getting started (5 minutes)

  1. Open the app (see the next two sections for the best way on your phone or computer).
  2. Tap Explore with sample data to look around with fictional numbers — or Add my first account to start for real.
  3. Add your accounts in Plan → Cashbook: bank accounts, cash, savings, e-wallets and credit cards, each with its current balance. (Tip: tick “do not count in my available cash” for savings/emergency accounts.)
  4. Set your payday in Plan → Payday, and your salary details in Plan → Salary → Salary settings.
  5. Add recurring bills, loan instalments, school/student-care fees and subscriptions with + → Bill / planned payment.
  6. Add debts in Plan → Debt and goals in Plan → Goals.
  7. When you're done exploring, remove the sample data: Home → ⚙︎ Settings → Remove sample data. Your own records are never touched.
  8. Make your first backup in Settings.

📱 Install on your iPhone or Android (recommended)

Why this matters: phones are not good at keeping data for an HTML file opened straight from the Files app or a chat (it may open in a preview that can't save). For reliable saving, offline use and a Home Screen icon, the app files need a web address. The files are just the empty app — your financial data never goes to the website; it stays in your phone's browser.

Free option: GitHub Pages (no cost, no ads)

  1. On a computer, create a free account at github.com.
  2. Click New repository. Give it a name, e.g. money. Choose Public (free Pages needs a public repository — this only makes the empty app code visible, never your data). Click Create.
  3. Click uploading an existing file and drag in everything from this folder except the source folder (you can include it; it's harmless). Click Commit changes.
  4. Go to the repository's Settings → Pages. Under “Build and deployment”, choose Deploy from a branch, branch main, folder / (root), then Save.
  5. After a minute or two the page shows your address, like https://yourname.github.io/money/.
  6. iPhone: open that address in Safari → tap Share → Add to Home Screen. Always open the app from that icon.
    Android: open it in Chrome → menu ⋮ → Install app / Add to Home screen.

Other free static hosts (e.g. Netlify or Cloudflare Pages free plans) work the same way. Avoid anything that asks for payment — none is needed.

Each browser keeps its own copy of the data. The Home Screen app on iPhone has its own storage too — so enter your data in the app you'll keep using, and use backups to move between them.

💻 Use on a computer

Double-click index.html to open it in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. Your data saves in that browser for that file location. Keep the file in the same folder (moving or renaming it can make the browser treat it as a new app — your data isn't lost, it's just tied to the old location; restore a backup to bring it across). Offline mode and installing work best from the web address described above.

🌷 Everyday use

  • + (centre button): add an expense, income, transfer, refund, reimbursement, balance adjustment, household settle-up, bill, account, goal, debt, or import.
  • Quick add: your most-used merchants appear at the top of the expense form. The app learns the category for each merchant.
  • Home: dashboard. Tap Customise dashboard to show, hide and reorder cards. Use the arrows to look at past months. The 👁 button hides all amounts (privacy mode).
  • Activity: History (filter, search, sort, custom dates), Upcoming (overdue, until payday, after payday, all planned payments), Calendar (tap a day to see everything on it).
  • Plan: Budget, Cashbook, Goals, Debt, Subscriptions, Salary, Bonus, Payday, Household, Business.
  • Insights: charts and reports. ⬇ exports CSV for the chosen period; 🖨 prints or saves a PDF.
  • Switch between All / Personal / Household / Business at the top of most screens.
  • Every transaction can be edited, duplicated or deleted (with Undo). Attach receipt photos under “More”.

🧮 How the maths works (so you can trust the numbers)

TransfersMoving money between your own accounts (including into savings) is never income or spending.
Credit cardsPurchases count as spending when made. Paying the card is a transfer to the card account, so it is not counted again. Card limits are never treated as cash.
RefundsLink a refund to the original expense — it reverses that spending in the same category. A refund can't exceed what's left of the original.
Available cashYour own accounts, excluding credit cards, joint accounts and anything you've marked “not in available cash”.
Planned payments“Mark paid” creates one transaction. If a matching transaction already exists (e.g. imported), you're offered to link it instead. Partial payments are supported; unpaid items carry forward as overdue until paid, deferred or cancelled. Deleting the payment reopens the bill.
HouseholdEach household expense records who paid and your share. Expenses paid by your husband don't change your balances. The app shows who owes whom; “Settle up” clears it without counting as income or spending.
BusinessSales/commissions/royalties are business income. Moving business money to your personal account is a transfer, so it isn't counted twice. Gross profit appears when product costs, raw materials or inventory are recorded.
DebtsEach payment is split into principal, interest and fees. Only principal (plus fees/interest that were added to the balance) reduces what you owe. “Update from statement” makes the lender's balance override the estimate. Credit Counselling Singapore arrangements are shown separately from licensed moneylender loans.
GoalsContributions can just be tracked, or move money with a transfer (not spending). If goals linked to one account add up to more than its balance, you get a warning.
SalaryAll rates are editable defaults — please check them against the CPF Board and your payslip. Defaults: employee CPF 20%, ordinary-wage ceiling S$8,000/month (employees 55 and below, 2026 — verify), overtime 1.5× of 12×basic÷(52×44). The NPL daily rate method is your choice. Estimates are always labelled; record actual payslips to compare.
BonusesExpected/confirmed bonuses are never counted as cash until you mark them received.
EstimatesPayday projections, daily food/transport estimates and “what-if” scenarios never change your records.

This app is a personal planning tool, not financial, tax or legal advice.

💾 Backups (please do this!)

  1. Open Home → ⚙︎ Settings → Back up (JSON). A file like purrse-backup_2026-11-26_1450.json downloads. It includes receipts.
  2. Keep it somewhere safe that you control (e.g. your own iCloud Drive / Google Drive folder, or a USB drive). The app reminds you if it's been more than 14 days.
  3. Encrypted backup locks the file with a password using AES-256. If you forget the password, nobody can open it — not even you.
  4. To restore: Settings → Restore backup → choose the file. You'll see what's inside before anything changes, and your current data is saved as a safety copy first.
Safety copy: before big changes (restoring, loading/removing sample data, deleting a goal or debt, erasing) the app keeps one safety copy. Settings → Restore safety copy takes you back.

🔁 Move to another device or browser

  1. On the old device: Settings → Back up. Send the file to yourself (AirDrop, email to yourself, your own cloud drive).
  2. On the new device: open the app (from your web address / Home Screen icon) → Restore backup → choose the file.

Backups made when the app was called Blush Money (blush-money-backup_….json) restore normally in Purrse.

There is no automatic sync between devices — that keeps your data private and free. Pick one main device for daily entries and use backups to move.

⬆️ Updating the app without losing data

  • Your data is stored separately from the app file. Replacing index.html with a newer version at the same address (same GitHub repository, or same file location on your computer) keeps all your records.
  • Always make a backup first.
  • On GitHub: open the repository → click index.html → upload the new file with the same name (Add file → Upload files) → Commit. Also change the version name in sw.js (e.g. purrse-v1.1.1) so phones pick up the update. Close and reopen the app (sometimes twice).
  • The app automatically upgrades older data formats when it opens (and backups from older versions restore fine).
  • Changing the app: edit the files in source/, then run python3 build.py to rebuild dist/index.html.

📥 Automatic transaction logging (what's honestly possible)

A web app cannot read your phone's notifications by itself. What it can do:

  • CSV statements: download a CSV from your bank → Import → CSV file → match the columns → review → save. Duplicates are detected and unticked.
  • Paste alerts: copy bank/SMS alerts → Import → Paste. The app reads amount, merchant, date and suggests a category and account (add the last 4 digits to your accounts to help matching).
  • Phone automation: iPhone Shortcuts or Android MacroDroid / Tasker can forward an alert's text to the app by opening https://your-address/#add=<alert text>. The alert lands in Import → Inbox for you to approve. The text never leaves your phone (the part after # is not sent to the website). Step-by-step instructions are in the app under Import → Phone setup. This only works when the app is opened from a web address. Trigger options differ by iOS/Android version, so I couldn't test them on your specific phone.
  • No bank logins or passwords are ever needed or stored.

🔒 Privacy & storage limits

  • Everything stays in your browser's private database on your device. No accounts, no servers, no analytics, no tracking, no ads.
  • Private/incognito windows erase data when closed — don't use them for this app.
  • Clearing website data for the app's address erases your records.
  • iPhone may clear data from websites you haven't used for a few weeks; adding the app to your Home Screen avoids that.
  • The app PIN only hides the screen. It is not encryption. Use your phone's passcode/Face ID and encrypted backups for real protection.
  • Settings shows how much space is used and whether the browser has agreed to keep the data.
